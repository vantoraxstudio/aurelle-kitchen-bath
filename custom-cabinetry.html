Custom Cabinetry & Storage

Cabinetry that carries the whole room.

Doors, drawers, grain and hardware set the character of a space. Planned well, they also make it quietly easy to live in.

Discuss Your Project

Design & Storage Philosophy

Plan for the things you own, not the things a catalogue assumes

Good storage begins with an inventory. How many pans? Which appliances come out daily? Where do towels, backup supplies and cleaning products belong?

From those answers we would plan proportions, drawer depths and internal fittings, so that every item is easy to reach and the room stays visually calm.

The options on this page are design inspiration. They are not guaranteed products or materials from a verified supplier.

Door Styles & Finishes

Material and colour inspiration

Door styles to consider

  • Flat slab: minimal, architectural and easy to wipe clean.
  • Shaker-inspired: a simple frame that suits both modern and traditional homes.
  • Ribbed or fluted: subtle texture that catches light.
  • Glazed: glass-fronted units for display and lightness.

Finishes to consider

  • Natural oak with visible grain for warmth
  • Painted finishes in muted sage or warm stone tones
  • Two-tone schemes that separate island from perimeter

Bathroom Vanities & Built-Ins

Calm storage for personal spaces

Vanities, mirror cabinets and linen towers keep daily items close without cluttering surfaces. In bathrooms, material choice matters: finishes need to handle humidity and frequent cleaning, which is worth discussing early.

Pantry & Organisation

Ideas for a calmer pantry

Zone by habit

Group breakfast, baking and snack items so they live where they are used.

Make depth usable

Pull-out trays and shallow shelves keep items visible instead of lost at the back.

Plan for bulk

Reserve space for larger containers and seasonal items so they do not spill into the main kitchen.

Light the inside

Interior lighting turns a deep cupboard into a space you actually enjoy opening.

Design Selection Process

How cabinetry decisions could be made

  1. Inventory and priorities

    List what needs to be stored, what is used daily, and what annoys you now.

  2. Layout and proportions

    Test cabinet sizes, drawer stacks and tall units against your measurements.

  3. Style and finish

    Review door profiles, colours and timber tones as samples in your own light.

  4. Hardware and details

    Choose handles, lighting and internal fittings that suit the palette.

  5. Confirm and document

    Record every decision clearly before work moves forward.
