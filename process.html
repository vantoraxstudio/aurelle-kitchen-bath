Our Process

A remodel is easier when you can see the road ahead.

Seven stages describe how a kitchen or bathroom project might progress, from the first conversation to the final walk-through.

This is a conceptual outline. Real projects vary, and no completion dates, outcomes or construction methods are promised.

The Seven Stages

From first conversation to final review

  1. Initial conversation

    A relaxed discussion about your home, your household and why you are considering a change. No decisions yet, just listening.

    You bring: photos, ideas, questions.

  2. Needs and goals

    We turn frustrations and wishes into a clear list of priorities, noting what is essential, what is preferred and what can wait.

    You bring: your must-haves and daily routines.

  3. Design direction

    Layout ideas, mood references and a palette direction are explored so you can react to something tangible.

    You bring: honest reactions, favourites and dislikes.

  4. Materials and finish selection

    Cabinetry, surfaces, tile, fittings and lighting are reviewed together, ideally as samples in your own light.

    You bring: time to view samples at home.

  5. Scope and planning

    Decisions are documented, scope is clarified and sequencing is discussed so everyone shares the same expectations.

    You bring: questions about scope, budget and practicalities.

  6. Construction coordination

    Work is coordinated and communicated in plain language, with a clear route for questions and any necessary changes.

    You bring: availability to answer questions promptly.

  7. Final review and handover

    A walk-through of the finished space, with care guidance and a chance to note anything that needs attention.

    You bring: a keen eye and your checklist.

Preparation

What you may want to prepare

A little preparation makes early conversations far more productive.

  • Photos of the current room from several angles
  • Images of kitchens or bathrooms you admire
  • A list of what works and what frustrates you today
  • Any measurements, plans or appliance ideas you already have
  • A sense of your priorities and your comfort with disruption

Communication Expectations

Staying informed without being overwhelmed

  • A single, agreed way to ask questions and share decisions
  • Written records of selections and changes
  • Clear explanations when something needs a decision
  • Honest discussion of trade-offs, not just good news

Before You Start

Questions worth considering

How do we use this room?

Think about weekdays, weekends and special occasions, and who uses the space.

What matters most?

Decide which priorities you would protect if choices became difficult.

How much disruption can we manage?

Consider temporary cooking or bathing arrangements and your daily schedule.

What would we regret skipping?

Small details such as lighting or storage often matter more than expected.
