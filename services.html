Services

Every service begins with a thoughtful plan.

Explore the kitchen, bathroom and cabinetry services imagined for AURELLE, and the problems each one is designed to solve.

Request a Consultation
01 — Kitchen Remodeling

Kitchens that work as well as they look

The problem: a layout that fights the way you cook, too little storage, or surfaces and lighting that never quite came together.

Why planning matters: placing the sink, hob, refrigerator and preparation space with intention reduces daily friction, and coordinating finishes early prevents mismatched compromises later.

Discover Kitchen Design
02 — Bathroom Remodeling

Bathrooms designed for calm and ease

The problem: cramped circulation, poor lighting, awkward storage or tile and fittings that feel dated or disconnected.

Why planning matters: wet areas reward careful thought about drainage, ventilation, light and cleaning, so that beauty and practicality support each other.

Explore Bathroom Design
03 — Custom Cabinetry

Cabinetry designed around your belongings

The problem: standard units that waste awkward corners, or storage that is hard to reach and harder to keep organised.

Why planning matters: listing what you own and how often you use it lets internal layouts, door styles and finishes be shaped around real needs.

Discuss Cabinetry

Supporting Services

The details that hold a project together

Storage & built-in solutions

Problem: clutter in rooms that were never designed to store what you own.

Pantries, linen cabinets, window seats and wall-to-wall units can be planned to follow the architecture of the room. Planning storage early lets it feel built-in rather than added on.

See storage ideas

Material & finish coordination

Problem: beautiful individual samples that do not work together once installed.

Stone, timber, tile, paint, hardware and lighting are reviewed as one palette, in your own light, so the finished room feels cohesive.

Design-Build Planning

An integrated approach, described as an illustrative offering

Design-build planning would bring design decisions and construction coordination into a single, connected conversation.

The idea is to reduce hand-offs: the people shaping the design stay in dialogue with the people planning the work, so practical realities are considered early. This is a concept description only, not a verified real-world capability, and no timelines, guarantees or savings are implied.
